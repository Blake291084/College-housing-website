How does the rising cost of housing affect college students?

This project investigates how students balance rent, tuition, food, transportation, work, and time while trying to stay enrolled.

01 / Research context

A housing decision is also a time decision.

As housing costs rise, college students must make difficult choices about where they live, how much they work, and what they can afford.

2.1%

Year-over-year rise in U.S. house prices reported by the Federal Housing Finance Agency, 2025–2026.

As of recent, the prices of housing have gone up quite substantially and because of this people are finding it hard to live and hard to balance their finances. The U.S. Federal Housing Finance Agency reports that just from 2025 to 2026 the house prices rose 2.1%. They also state that the housing market has been rising ever since 2012. These rising prices make finding somewhere decent to live hard since all the prices keep going up like crazy.

And while your everyday person is struggling with this, it is also hitting college students harder. In college you have to not only balance your budget, but you also must balance your time wisely. While in college you have to work a job, go to class, study for said class, and maybe just maybe have some free time in between.

But while trying to put your all into classes and get good grades you also must put in an ample amount of time into a job in order to be able to afford a housing unit and to pay tuition, and to also feed yourself and other small things in-between. But in order to get enough money you need to put in enough time, but your time is filled with going to class, doing schoolwork, and having to study for tests.

As you can see this loop is already so confusing and convoluted, so imagine going through this lifestyle every day. With housing prices rising it becomes increasingly difficult for students to be able to earn that money whether it be because of a low-paying job or because they just can’t fit in enough time to make enough money to live off of.

04 / Reflection

“Students still struggle to balance paying their tuition, paying their rent, paying for food and gas and more.”

My first assumption was that housing farther from campus would be noticeably cheaper. The comparison complicates that idea: Wildwood is the least expensive option, but U Lofts is similarly priced while much closer to campus. Meanwhile, Wildwood’s shuttle access may lower the cost of transportation and help account for its distance.

This shows that a student’s decision cannot be measured by rent alone. The amount of time spent commuting, the cost of gas, access to transportation, and the pressure to work enough hours all matter alongside the listed monthly price.
